## 3. İlk 5 topluluk cevabı (Reddit / Quora)
**Kurallar:** sahip kendi hesabından, kendi sözleriyle düzenleyerek paylaşır. Reddit'te her alt forumun kuralları okunur; bağlantıya izin yoksa bağlantısız paylaşılır. Şeffaflık cümlesi silinmez. Aynı metin iki yerde kullanılmaz.

### Cevap 1 — Quora: "How many days do I need in Istanbul for a first visit?"
> Three full days is the minimum I'd suggest, five is comfortable. More than the number of days, the order matters:
>
> **Day 1 — the historic peninsula.** Hagia Sophia at opening time, the Blue Mosque, the Hippodrome, the Basilica Cistern, and Topkapı Palace in the afternoon (give it three hours; it's usually closed on Tuesdays).
> **Day 2 — bazaars and the Bosphorus.** Grand Bazaar in the morning (closed Sundays), walk down to the Spice Bazaar via Süleymaniye Mosque, then a Bosphorus boat from Eminönü in the afternoon.
> **Day 3 — Galata and the Asian side.** Galata Tower, İstiklal Avenue, then the 20-minute ferry to Kadıköy for dinner.
>
> The most common mistake is trying to do the whole historic peninsula in half a day. The second is taking taxis everywhere — the T1 tram and the ferries are usually faster.
>
> Disclosure: I run a small travel advisory in Istanbul. I wrote the full 3/5/7-day version here if it helps: https://itineraryofturkiye.com/blog/istanbul-itinerary-3-5-7-days

### Cevap 2 — Reddit r/istanbul veya r/travel: "First time in Istanbul, any scams to watch out for?"
> Istanbul is safe and people are genuinely helpful, but a few tricks target first-timers:
>
> - **Taxi meter / route.** Insist on the meter or use BiTaksi/Uber (both work with official yellow taxis).
> - **Banknote switch.** You hand over a large note, it becomes a small one. Say the amount out loud when you pay.
> - **Dropped shoe-shine brush.** Someone drops a brush, you pick it up, a "free" shine turns into a bill. Just say no thanks and keep walking.
> - **The friendly stranger + bar.** Mostly around Taksim/Sultanahmet, mostly aimed at men. Pick your own bars.
> - **"The mosque is closed, come see my cousin's shop."** Check the entrance yourself.
> - **Fish "by weight" with no price.** Ask the price before ordering.
>
> Save 112 — it's the single emergency number.
>
> (I work in trip planning in Istanbul, so I hear these stories a lot. [Kurallar izin veriyorsa:] Longer write-up: https://itineraryofturkiye.com/blog/turkey-tourist-scams-how-to-avoid)

### Cevap 3 — Quora: "What is the best month to visit Turkey?"
> For most first-time visitors: **May or September.** Both are warm without the summer heat, the crowds are lower than July–August, and you can combine Istanbul, Cappadocia and the coast in one trip. In September the sea is at its warmest.
>
> It depends on where you're going though:
> - Istanbul: April–May, September–October.
> - Cappadocia: April–June, September–October (balloon flights are most stable then).
> - The coast (Antalya, Bodrum, Fethiye): late May–June, September–October.
> - The southeast (Göbekli Tepe, Mardin, Gaziantep): October–April — summers go past 40°C.
> - The Black Sea and the east: June–September.
>
> Also check the dates of Ramadan and the two Bayram holidays for your year — domestic travel peaks during the holidays.
>
> Disclosure: I'm part of a travel advisory in Istanbul. Month-by-month breakdown: https://itineraryofturkiye.com/blog/best-time-to-visit-turkiye-month-by-month

### Cevap 4 — Reddit r/Turkey veya r/travel: "Is Cappadocia worth it if the balloon flight gets cancelled?"
> Yes. The balloons are the headline, but the substance of Cappadocia is on the ground: sunrise from the viewpoints (the balloons are just as beautiful from below), walking the Love, Rose or Red valleys early before the heat, the rock-cut churches at the Göreme Open-Air Museum, and the underground cities at Derinkuyu or Kaymaklı.
>
> Practical tip: plan at least two possible balloon mornings. Flights are cancelled when wind or visibility is unsafe, and a good operator will rebook you — ask for the cancellation and refund policy in writing before paying. Very cheap offers are worth a second look.
>
> 2–3 nights is the sweet spot. Göreme is central; Uçhisar and Ortahisar are quieter.

### Cevap 5 — Quora: "Is public transport in Istanbul easy for tourists?"
> Yes, and it's usually faster than taxis. Get an **Istanbulkart** from the machines at any metro or tram station (contactless bank cards also work at many gates). Three lines cover most visits:
>
> - **T1 tram:** Kabataş – Karaköy – Eminönü – Sultanahmet – Grand Bazaar. This is the tourist line.
> - **M2 metro:** Taksim, Şişli, Levent.
> - **Marmaray:** rail under the Bosphorus between the European and Asian sides.
>
> And the ferries — both transport and the cheapest Bosphorus view you'll get.
>
> Avoid the M2, T1 and Metrobus at weekday rush hours (roughly 7:00–9:30 and 16:30–19:30) if you can.
>
> Disclosure: I work in trip planning in Istanbul. Full guide: https://itineraryofturkiye.com/blog/istanbul-public-transport-tourist-guide

### The Order Matters More Than the Itinerary: What First-Time Visitors to Istanbul Get Wrong

Most people planning their first trip to Istanbul ask the same question: *how many days do I need?* It is the wrong first question. When we plan trips for first-time visitors, the number of days matters far less than the order in which you spend them.

**Istanbul punishes the wrong order.** It is a city of nearly 16 million people spread over two continents, with traffic that can turn a 20-minute drive into an hour. The famous sights are clustered, but the city's character is spread out. If you get the sequence wrong, you spend your trip in queues and taxis. Get it right and the same three days feel like a week.

**Start where everyone starts — but early.** The historic peninsula of Sultanahmet holds Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, the Basilica Cistern and Topkapı Palace within a few minutes' walk of each other. That is exactly why it is crowded. Arrive at opening time and you will have Hagia Sophia's upper gallery almost to yourself for twenty minutes. Arrive at noon and you will spend that time in a line. Remember that these are working mosques: visits pause for prayers, and Friday midday is the busiest moment of the week.

**Do not try to see it all in half a day.** This is the single most common mistake we see. Four major monuments, each with its own entrance and its own rhythm, are a full day. Topkapı alone deserves three hours — and it is usually closed on Tuesdays, which catches out more visitors than you would think.

**Let the water show you the city.** On the second day, walk through the Grand Bazaar, then downhill through the market streets to the Spice Bazaar, and board a boat at Eminönü. Istanbul only makes sense from the Bosphorus: palaces, fortresses and wooden mansions slide past between Europe and Asia, and you finally understand the shape of the place.

**Cross to the other side.** The Asian side is twenty minutes away by public ferry, and most first-time visitors never go. Kadıköy is where Istanbul's residents actually eat, shop and spend their evenings. It is the quickest cure for the feeling that you have only seen a museum.

**Use the tram, not the taxi.** The T1 tram runs straight through the historic centre, the ferries cross the Bosphorus in minutes, and neither is affected by traffic. Taxis still have their place — with luggage, late at night, with elderly relatives — but they should not be your default.

**Three days, in the right order:**
1. The historic peninsula, starting at opening time.
2. Bazaars on foot, then the Bosphorus by boat.
3. Galata and İstiklal, then the ferry to Kadıköy for the evening.

Five days lets you add Balat's colourful streets and the Bosphorus villages; seven lets you add the car-free Princes' Islands or an overnight trip to Bursa or Edirne.

The best Istanbul trips we plan are not the ones that fit the most in. They are the ones where every day starts in the right place.
